# 08 — Selling to Thai Business Owners 🤝

This is the part that decides whether the business lives or dies. The product is easy (AI builds it). **Winning trust is the real job** — and Thai business culture has specific rules. The good news: a warm, polite **Thai woman is the ideal person** to do this.

## Channel ranking for reaching Thai hotel owners

| Rank | Channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **In-person visit** (if convenient) | Highest trust by far. A polite drop-in with a free Maps audit + small gift beats 10 emails. Optional here, but gold when possible. |
| 2 | **LINE** | The default for *all* Thai business. ~56M users, ~94% open rate. Where deals actually get done. **But:** you can't cold-message strangers — they must add you first. So LINE is the **warm/follow-up** channel. |
| 3 | **Facebook Page / Messenger DM** | Most small hotels live on Facebook and check Messenger. A personal (not copy-paste) DM gets read. Doubles as proof: "your FB is great but you've no website." |
| 4 | **Phone call** to the hotel's main number | Culturally OK for first contact if polite + brief; then pivot to LINE. |
| 5 | **Referrals / introductions** | A *multiplier* on everything. A warm intro from another owner/supplier/tourism-association contact converts 2–4× better. Make this your long-game. |
| 6 | **Email** | Last resort. <15% open rate; Thais distrust promo email. Use for formal quotes/contracts *after* warming up. Never your main cold channel. |

> **Reality:** cold *email alone* barely works here. The winning combo is **Facebook DM or phone → move to LINE → build the relationship → close on LINE/in person.** (Templates in `templates/outreach/`.)

## The culture rules (DO / DON'T)

**DO**
- Open with genuine small talk (the hotel, the area, family) **before** business.
- Address them **"Khun" + first name**; use the *wai* greeting; a little Thai goes a long way (she's a native speaker — huge advantage).
- Be **patient** — expect 2–4 touches before a yes. Follow up ~weekly with *value* (a tip, a spotted issue), not pressure.
- Send a **one-page Thai-language summary**; visuals reduce misunderstanding and signal seriousness.
- Use **local social proof:** "A guesthouse just like yours in [same town] increased direct bookings — here's their new site."
- Let them "win" a little on price; agree gracefully. Maintain the relationship after the sale (monthly check-in, proactive reports) — Thai loyalty (*bunkhun*) runs deep once trust is earned.

**DON'T**
- Don't open with a hard pitch, price, or "limited-time!" pressure — it kills trust instantly.
- Don't make them lose face: never correct/challenge publicly, never push ultimatums.
- Don't read politeness as a "yes." Because of **kreng jai** (not wanting to cause awkwardness), a Thai owner rarely says "no" — they go quiet, vague, or "I'll think about it." That usually *means no* → gently probe for the real worry.
- Don't badmouth Agoda/competitors directly — frame as "what we add," not "they're bad."
- Don't spam (daily messages), don't message late at night early in the relationship, don't assume the person you met can decide alone (family businesses consult).

## The message that resonates (what to actually say)
Lead with **one specific, true observation + a quick win + a soft ask:**
1. **OTA pain:** "You're paying Agoda ~20% — a direct-booking site keeps that money."
2. **Found on Google:** "Your Google Maps is missing photos and has no website link — guests can't find or book you directly."
3. **Trust for foreign guests:** "A clean English/Thai/Chinese site makes foreign guests confident to book."
4. **Local Thai support:** "I'm Thai, here, and you can reach me on LINE anytime" — a real edge over foreign DIY tools (Wix/Squarespace).

**Best opener = a free 2-minute audit video** of *their* Google listing/site, pointing out 2–3 fixable gaps. It's specific, generous (activates reciprocity), and converts far better than a generic pitch.

## LINE Official Account (your own, for the business)
- Free to create; free tier ~300–500 broadcasts/month; Basic ~฿1,200–1,500/mo for ~10–15k messages.
- Use it for: sending quotes, follow-ups, ongoing client support, and (later) broadcasting tips to your client list. **Get a QR code** on your one-pager so prospects add you in one tap.
- You **cannot** cold-blast strangers on LINE — get them to add you via the QR/Facebook/phone first.

## Pricing & negotiation norms
- Expect haggling: **quote ~10–15% above your floor**, leave room to "give" them a discount gracefully.
- **Deposit 50% up front, balance before go-live** is normal and expected. A small entry price or short trial lowers friction for cash-tight owners.
- Never look like you *need* the sale; patience is leverage here.

## Building the referral flywheel (the real growth engine)
1. Win 2–3 reference clients in **one town/segment**.
2. Get a short testimonial + permission to show their result.
3. Ask each happy client: "Do you know another owner who'd want this?" (warm intros are the norm).
4. Consider a small **referral thank-you** (a free month, a gift) — culturally natural.
5. Join local **tourism associations / owner Facebook groups** to seed intros.
