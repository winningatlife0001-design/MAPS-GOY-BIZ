# 07 — Legal, Tax & the Foreigner Question ⚠️

> **This is general information, not legal advice.** Thai law here is real and actively enforced. The few places that genuinely need a Thai lawyer or accountant are flagged 🧑‍⚖️. A one-hour paid consult before you scale is cheap insurance. Sources: Tilleke & Gibbins, Revenue Department, ASEAN Briefing, Belaws, Acclime, PwC, Chambers (researched June 2026).

---

## The headline

✅ **A Thai national can legally run this business solo. That's the clean, correct structure.**
⚠️ **A foreigner doing the actual work — even unpaid, even from behind the scenes — is where the legal danger is.**
✅ **Using AI (Claude) to produce the work is fine — it's a software tool, not an employee.**

So the safe model is simple: **[girlfriend] genuinely owns and runs it** (decisions, money, client relationships); **AI tools do production**; **the boyfriend stays out of operations.**

---

## 1) How [girlfriend] should set up (Thai national)

- **Start as a sole proprietor** with a **commercial registration (ทะเบียนพาณิชย์)** at the local district office / via DBD. Required once you're trading a service (the income trigger is tiny). Cheap, simple, one person. *(Only Thai nationals — or US citizens under the Amity Treaty — can be sole proprietors. Perfect here.)*
- **Don't bother with a limited company at first** — it needs 3 shareholders, monthly filings, and real overhead. Form one later *only* if a big client requires invoicing a company, or you want the foreigner to legally invest (see §4).
- DBD registration is now **online** (mandatory online for companies from 1 Jan 2026).

## 2) Tax (sole proprietor)

| Tax | Rate / rule | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal income tax** | Progressive **0%→35%** | 0% up to ฿150k net; 5% to ฿300k; 10% to ฿500k; 15% to ฿750k; 20% to ฿1M; 25% to ฿2M; 30% to ฿5M; 35% above. File **PND 90 by end of March.** |
| **Expense deduction** | Service income (S.40(2)): **50% of income, capped at ฿100,000** | A tax accountant may classify some work differently (S.40(6)) — 🧑‍⚖️ confirm. |
| **Personal allowance** | ฿60,000 | Standard. |
| **VAT** | **7%**, mandatory only once revenue exceeds **฿1,800,000/year** | Below ฿1.8M you don't charge or file VAT — a normal receipt is fine. Register within 30 days of crossing. |
| **Withholding tax (WHT)** | **3%** on service fees | When a *company* client pays you, they usually withhold 3% and remit it; you credit it against your income tax. Keep the WHT certificates. |
| **Social security** | Voluntary (Section 40): **฿70 / ฿100 / ฿300 per month** | Optional for the self-employed; only becomes mandatory (Section 33, 5%) once you hire an employee. |

**Practical:** until you're earning ฿1.8M+/year, life is simple — registered sole proprietor, keep receipts, file personal tax in March. Get a cheap Thai accountant once money flows.

## 3) ⚠️ The foreigner question (the critical part)

Thai law defines **"work" extremely broadly:** *"any activity exerting physical or mental energy, whether or not for wages or other benefits."* That includes **unpaid, advisory, and behind-the-scenes** work.

| The boyfriend... | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lives **outside Thailand**, gives **informal personal support/advice** to his girlfriend, no role/title/contract | **Lower risk / grey area** | The law is territorial (work "performed in Thailand"). No clear ruling on pure remote advice from abroad. Not settled — 🧑‍⚖️ get an opinion before relying on it at scale. |
| **Physically in Thailand** doing *any* business task (emails, client calls, design review, "just helping") **without a work permit** | **Illegal** | Counts as "work." Fines ฿5,000–50,000, deportation, work-permit ban. 2024–25 saw 38,000+ inspections; "online content" work is being targeted. |
| Acts as the **real operator** while girlfriend is just the legal "front" (**nominee**) | **Illegal for BOTH** | Nominee arrangements breach the Foreign Business Act + Alien Working Act. Thai nominee fine ฿100k–฿1M; foreigner: fine + deportation + criminal charges. |
| Holds **≤49%** shares as a genuine minority investor in a Thai-majority company | **Legal** | A ≥51% Thai company isn't "foreign" under the FBA. But from 1 Jan 2026, the Thai shareholder must show **3 months of bank statements** proving the capital is genuinely theirs (anti-nominee rule). |
| Holds **≥50%** of a service company | **Restricted** | Web design/marketing fall under the FBA **List 3 "advertising / other services"** restriction; a foreign-majority service company needs a hard-to-get Foreign Business Licence. Avoid. |
| Gets a **proper work permit** (Non-B visa, company sponsor) | **Legal** | The legitimate way for a foreigner to actually work in Thailand — but needs a company of sufficient size/capital to sponsor it. Overkill at the start. |

### What this means in plain English
- **The cleanest, safest setup:** [girlfriend] is the genuine owner-operator. She talks to clients, makes decisions, signs things, gets paid into her account. **AI does the production work.** The boyfriend is a *supportive partner*, not a worker — no title, no client-facing role, no "running it from abroad."
- **Is using Claude a problem?** No. AI software is a tool the Thai owner uses (like Photoshop or Wix). The legal risk isn't the AI — it's a *foreigner* doing the labour or secretly controlling the business.
- 🧑‍⚖️ **Two things genuinely worth a lawyer's hour** before scaling: (1) confirming the remote-advice line so the boyfriend stays clearly on the safe side of "not working/not a nominee," and (2) if you ever want him to invest money, doing the ≤49% structure correctly with the new bank-statement rule. Recommended firms: Tilleke & Gibbins, Siam Legal, Belaws, Acclime.

## 4) If you later want the boyfriend to invest (optional)
Form a Thai limited company, **Thai majority ≥51%**, foreigner **≤49%** as a genuine minority shareholder with genuinely his own funds, keep him **out of day-to-day work** unless he gets a work permit. Do this with an incorporation lawyer because of the 2026 anti-nominee bank-statement rule. Not needed to start.

## 5) PDPA (data protection) — for outreach & client data
- Thailand's **PDPA** is GDPR-style and **now actively fined** (฿21.5M+ in Aug 2025). It protects **individuals'** personal data.
- **Cold outreach:** safest to contact the **business** (generic `info@`, `reservations@`, the hotel's public phone/Facebook page) rather than a named person's private email. Always include an **easy opt-out** ("reply STOP / no thanks"). Keep volumes reasonable. (B2B-to-a-company sits in a defensible zone; messaging a named individual's personal data is riskier.)
- **Client & guest data:** when we handle a hotel's customer enquiries/bookings, keep a short privacy note on their site, store data securely, and don't reuse it. 🧑‍⚖️ A data adviser can review your consent/notice once before you scale email campaigns.

## 6) Quick compliance checklist
- [ ] [girlfriend] registers as a sole proprietor (ทะเบียนพาณิชย์).
- [ ] Open a **business bank account in her name**; all client money flows there.
- [ ] Keep every receipt/invoice; file personal tax (PND 90) each March.
- [ ] Watch the **฿1.8M/year** line → register for VAT if you cross it.
- [ ] Boyfriend: **no title, no client-facing work, no operating from inside Thailand** without a permit.
- [ ] Add a simple **privacy notice** to client sites; offer opt-out in all outreach.
- [ ] 🧑‍⚖️ Before scaling: 1-hour lawyer consult (foreigner line + structure) + cheap accountant.

*Get this structure right once and you can stop worrying about it. It's genuinely fine for a Thai person to run this — the whole design of the business (Thai owner + AI production) is built to keep it clean.*
