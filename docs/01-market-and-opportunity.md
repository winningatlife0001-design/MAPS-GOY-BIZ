# 01 — Market & Opportunity 📊

*All figures researched June 2026, cited. Confidence noted where it matters.*

## The big picture

- Thailand is one of the world's top tourism economies. **2024: 35.55M international arrivals, ~฿1.8 trillion revenue.** 2025 dipped to **32.97M (-7.2%)** (mainly China down -34%), and 2026 is targeted at **30–34M.** [TAT / Bangkok Post / Nation Thailand]
- A softer year actually *helps our pitch*: when arrivals dip, hotels feel OTA commissions more and want **direct bookings** + better visibility. Pain = motivation to buy.
- **Malaysia is now the #1 source market** (4.52M), just ahead of China (4.47M), then **India (2.49M, fastest-growing)**, Russia, South Korea. Translation: target sites need **English + Thai + Chinese**, and Indian/Russian markets matter too.

## How many potential customers?

- **17,126 registered hotels / accommodation businesses** (711,729 rooms, H1 2024). [Krungsri / LH Bank]
- Since 2023, properties with **≤8 rooms no longer need a full hotel license**, which legalized a huge layer of small guesthouses. Including informal places, the realistic addressable pool is **~25,000–35,000 accommodation businesses.** (estimate, Med confidence)
- Beyond hotels: **3.2 million MSMEs** in Thailand (99.5% of all firms). [World Bank / OSMEP] Our adjacent targets (below) add hundreds of thousands more.

## The core problem we solve (this is the wedge)

- Independent Thai hotels run on OTAs: typical **direct-booking share is only 8–15%**, i.e. **85–92% of bookings go through Agoda/Booking.com.** [The Percentage Co.; academic studies — Med-High]
- **OTA commission: Agoda 15–25%, Booking 15–18% (effectively up to ~28% with programs).** [HospitalityNet; Cloudbeds — High]
- Most small hotels' "website" is a **Facebook page** and their **OTA listings**. They often have **no owned site** and a **30–50% incomplete Google profile.**
- Structural cause: **only 11.9% of Thai firms innovate** (vs 37–41% in Vietnam/Malaysia/Philippines). [World Bank — High] Owners are busy, non-technical, and language-shy — exactly the gap a friendly Thai-speaking service fills.

## 🎁 The limited-time gift: 200% SME digital tax deduction

- Thailand approved a **200% corporate income tax deduction for SMEs on qualifying digital spending**, **capped at ฿300,000/year, active 24 June 2025 → 31 December 2027.** [Lexnova, Optimum, Mahanakorn — High]
- Eligibility: paid-up capital **≤฿5M** and revenue **≤฿30M** (i.e. almost every small hotel that's a registered company).
- Qualifying spend includes **software and DEPA-approved digital services** (400+ products in the Thailand Digital Catalog).
- **Why this is gold for sales:** a registered-company hotel can effectively write off **double** the cost of digital services against tax. "Get a website *and* a tax break, but only until end of 2027" is a real, honest urgency hook. (Tell them to confirm eligibility with their accountant — and note a *sole-proprietor* hotel files personal, not corporate, tax, so this specific deduction is a company-client angle.)

## Where to hunt (best regions, ranked)

**Tier 1 (start here — high density of small independents + international guests):**
- **Phuket** (Kata, Patong, Rawai, Kamala) — Southern region has the most properties (4,280 establishments); high-value European/Russian/Aussie guests → strong direct-booking ROI.
- **Chiang Mai** — 2,400+ small hotels/guesthouses + big shadow inventory; budget/mid independents; digital-nomad support ecosystem.
- **Bangkok** — huge count of independent guesthouses (Sukhumvit, Khao San, Silom); price-competitive → commission savings bite.
- **Pattaya / East coast** — highest occupancy (83.9% Dec 2024); Russian/Eastern-European market books off lesser-known channels.

**Tier 2 (strong):** Krabi / Koh Lanta / Railay · Koh Samui / Koh Phangan · Hua Hin (Hua Hin is domestic-heavy → lead with **Thai-language** direct-booking sites).

**Tier 3 (blue-ocean, low budget but almost zero competition):** Pai (100+ guesthouses, mostly Facebook-only) · Chiang Rai · Isaan (Khon Kaen, Udon, Ubon).

> Practical tip: **dominate one town at a time.** Become "the website person for guesthouses in Ao Nang," collect 2–3 local reference clients, then referrals do the selling (huge in Thai culture — see `08`).

## Adjacent segments (same playbook, more customers)

| Segment | Size signal | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Restaurants / cafés** | 321k+ restaurants/cafés; ~500k food establishments by 2024 | 88% of diners check menu online first; most are Facebook-only; want online menu + reservations + Maps |
| **Spas / wellness / massage** | **92,813 wellness operators**, ฿670B sector | Tourist-facing, visual, bookings-driven; love Google reviews |
| **Dive shops** | Koh Tao alone 50+ centres; diving market growing 12%/yr | Foreign customers, booking-led, Facebook/Maps only |
| **Clinics: dental / cosmetic / medical** | 3M medical tourists/yr; dental tourism booming (~21% CAGR) | High value per customer; will pay well for patient acquisition |
| **Tour operators** | tens of thousands of micro-operators | Itinerary + instant-book + Google Things-to-Do |

Hotels are the **beachhead** (clearest ROI story). The same site + Maps service resells to all of the above with minor tweaks.

## Willingness to pay — the honest read

- No public survey of small-hotel digital budgets exists. Constraints are real: tight cash flow in a down year, non-technical family owners, language. (Med confidence)
- But the **ROI math is unusually concrete** (commission savings), and the **tax deduction lowers the effective price**. The winning move is to **anchor every price to their OTA bill**, keep monthly fees **under ~฿5,000** (the comfort ceiling), and **show a similar local hotel's result** (social proof is everything in Thailand).

## Bottom line
Tens of thousands of motivated, under-served, OTA-squeezed small businesses; near-zero delivery cost because AI does production; a government tax subsidy with a 2027 deadline; and almost no competitor bundling **website + Google Maps + Thai-language care** the way we will. The opportunity is real. Execution = consistent selling + not screwing up the legal structure.
